Muffin is at risk due to behavioral reasons. Despite our best efforts at keeping Muffin comfortable while in care, he has been observed to show high levels of stress while in kennel. His kennel is frequently observed to be fingerpainted with feces. Muffin has also been observed to be rope reactive. Noted to jump up, bite, and tug on the leash. During one instance when a handler straight armed him due to the leash biting, Muffin attempted to bite the handler's sleeve. With handlers Muffin has been observed to be social and will lean into them during contact. Medically, Muffin is underweight.

A Volunteer writes: Is there a better word for spectacular? This dreamboat is an example of all that is good in the world: He adores everyone (he lived with two cats! He's friendly with other dogs!); his affection with humans knows no bounds, he adores bath time, squeaky toys, and showing off his basic commands. In the shelter, this love Muffin (can't help it) has stolen our hearts with his unbelievable smile and the fact that he makes everyone feel good about themselves and the world with his endless cheer. As one volunteer says, "He's the sweetest, gentlest, soulful boy." All that and more is waiting for you with Muffin!

Muffin came into ACC as an owner surrender. He was surrendered due to his owner not having enough time to take care of him. During his time in the care center, he has been noted to have significant kennel reactivity and has been noted to finger paint feces in his kennel. He has also been noted to intensely leash bite while in kennel and being very hard to redirect this behavior. He is noted to be a volunteer favorite and is social with staff, enjoying pets and leaning into them. We recommend placement with a New Hope partner who can provide any necessary behavior modification (force-free, positive reinforcement-based) and re-evaluate behavior in a stable home environment before placement into a permanent home.
