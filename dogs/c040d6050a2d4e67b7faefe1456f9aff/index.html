HISTORY

Zandoli was rescued from Dominica, where good Samaritans found her injured, with a broken leg and other minor injuries that suggested she had been hit by a car. She was treated at the local shelter and has since fully healed. She traveled to Brooklyn in late-June 2026.

DETAILED PROFILE

Back home in Dominica, the zandoli is a small, quick lizard found on every wall and garden, and considered a sign of good luck. It's easy to see why this Zandoli earned the name: she showed up, charmed everyone around her, and within days had her foster impressed by how fast she settled in.

Goofy, intelligent, and entertaining are the words that come up most, and she's a remarkably quick study. Handed an improvised bed of pillows one afternoon, she claimed it before it reached the floor, and it's been her spot since. She sits when she senses the moment, even without the word, and reads context easily: off the couch, leave that, come here.

In a busy home with two young kids, she's shown a rare balance. She matches the energy when things get lively, engages with the children when they seek her out, then takes herself to bed for quiet when she's had enough. Food-motivated as she is, she doesn't beg at the table, stays relaxed around her bowl even with kids nearby, takes treats politely from small hands, and doesn't bark.

She's a crate star who settles quietly overnight and is largely house trained, with only a couple of early, transition-related accidents. On leash she's easy, more interested in a passing squirrel than in other dogs. Her experience living with dogs and cats is still unknown, though so far she's curious and mostly polite meeting dogs on leash.

Off the clock she's a cuddler with a growing love of toys and a whippet's gift for happy high-speed circles before flopping back into lounge mode. When she wants affection, she asks softly, with eye contact and her head in your hand. The early read is a silly, affectionate, quick-learning girl who takes everything, couch tumbles included, in stride.
