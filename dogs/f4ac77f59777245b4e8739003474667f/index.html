Pre-Screener Form

Place with A New Hope Partner: Roxanne came into ACC as a stray; because of this we do not know how she would be in a home environment. During her time in the care center, Roxanne has been noted to have significant reactivity to dogs, lunging and snarling towards adjacent kennels and dogs in crates. This behavior is not consistently redirectable, but at times she is able to be distracted with a toy. Roxanne has also been noted to have a low threshold for arousal, in one instance she became frustrated during a training session and jumped up with an open mouth towards the staff members armpit. She is social with handlers, leaning in for petting and resting her front legs on handler's laps with a loose wiggly body. We recommend placement with a New Hope partner who can provide any necessary behavior modification (force-free, positive reinforcement-based) and re-evaluate behavior in a stable home environment before placement into a permanent home.

A Volunteer Wrote: This magical girl, who has quickly become a staff and volunteer favorite, is the definition of resilience (and sweetness) under adversity: She's not only been living in a pop up crate, but a pop up crate right inside the entrance to the shelter, never getting a moment of quiet. And yet: She sleep sweetly inside until it is time to go out, when she continues being her heart-achingly perfect self. Amazing on a leash, totally unreactive to other dogs in the park (including one who lunged and growled at her), and when she puts her head on your knee and rests it there to be pet (one of her favorite tricks), we promise your heart will be hers. She also plays with the best of them--see if you can find the video of her trying to eat the bubbles that someone blew for her! Better yet, bring her home and do it yourself, and revel in love with our dear Roxanne!
