HISTORY

Blackberry was found as a stray in Georgia, underweight, pregnant, and dealing with mange and skin infections; three weeks later she gave birth to a litter of 11 puppies, one of several litters she has had. Her owners declined to reclaim her. She traveled to Brooklyn in mid-October 2026.

DETAILED PROFILE

Blackberry cuddles like she means it. With a new friend, she starts with a polite lean, then stretches out one leg, then another, until she’s fully draped across them. She’s a sweet, wiggly mush who considers every bed and couch fair game.

She’s an adult who never got the memo. She plays like a puppy, does tippy taps at dinnertime, and once came sprinting in the second a Zumba video started. A frisbee is her current favorite toy, and the promise of a walk is pure joy. Her energy is moderate: regular walks and play, then a solid snooze.

Her social calendar is full. She wants to meet and play with every dog she sees, rolls belly-up for the bossier ones, and is over the moon when her foster’s dog finally plays back. She ignores the household cat and has been caught napping back-to-back with it. She’s met kids out and about, including a crowd of trick-or-treaters, and is expected to do well with kids, though she hasn’t spent much time around very young children.

She’s still settling in, and alone time is the big project. Being confined, whether in a crate or a closed room, really stresses her right now: she pants and drools heavily, paws at the door, and once squeezed out of a crate that wasn’t fully latched. She’ll likely do best with someone who can build up time alone gradually and isn’t away for long stretches at first. She’s mostly house-trained, with occasional accidents during stressful moments. On leash she’s a dedicated sniffer who would rather greet every dog than keep walking. She knows sit, stay, leave it, come, and off, and is still practicing that last one with new people.

Her ideal person is around a lot and wants a lap full of dog.

MEDICAL HISTORY

The dogs in our care are up-to-date on vaccinations, spayed or neutered (when old enough), microchipped, and given a general health check. Immediate medical needs and medical history will be addressed with the adopter prior to adoption. Complete vet records will be sent shortly after adoption.
