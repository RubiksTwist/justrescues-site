ABOUT ME: Some dogs collect sticks. I collect babies: stuffed ones, plus the occasional hat or eye mask that smells like my person. I’m a gentle, chatty senior with a whole vocabulary of squeaks, whines, and let’s-go barks, and I’ll howl along with any siren or smoke alarm that needs backup. New people line up to pet me, and I let them. Head scratches are my love language. Turns out the best field research happens from a couch.

MY FOSTER SAYS: "At the adoption event, this girl basically had me laying down and spooning her in order to get as many belly rubs as she possibly could. She looks up at you with those big eyes and it's completely irresistible."

HISTORY

Jane was found as a stray in Selma, Alabama. While her full history is unknown, it was evident that she had experienced neglect and needed significant medical attention and TLC. She arrived in Brooklyn in late January 2026.

DETAILED PROFILE

Jane Goodall keeps a collection. Stuffed animals, mostly, carried from room to room like a doting parent and tucked beside her wherever she lies down. Hats and eye masks that smell like her person have been known to join the pile. She doesn’t play with any of it, exactly. She just likes it close.

She’s a gentle senior with low-to-moderate energy: happy on a real walk, happier still stretched out for head scratches, with a burst of morning pep now that the weather has cooled. She’s also a talker, with a whole vocabulary of squeaks, whines, and let’s-go barks, plus the occasional howl at a passing siren. Mostly she’s calm, but if her person yelps in surprise, she’s up and on duty in a flash.

People are her favorite subject. She works an adoption-event line like a red carpet, has fans at local coffee shops, and won over a friend of her foster who is nervous around dogs. A neighborhood kid now asks her foster when Jane's next walk will be. She’s lived calmly with dogs in several foster homes, more relaxed housemate than playmate, and has shared homes peacefully with cats.

She’s house trained, crate trained, and excellent on leash, unbothered by trucks, motorcycles, and thunderstorms. She’ll settle in a crate, though she’d much rather be out with her people. She’s a pro in the car and was a model client at her first grooming appointment. She has some ongoing medical needs, and we’ll share the full picture with interested adopters. She’s also eligible for our foster-to-adopt program.

Fair warning: anything soft in your home will eventually end up in her collection.

MEDICAL HISTORY

Jane is up-to-date on required vaccinations, spayed and microchipped. She tested positive for heartworm and has not been treated.

She has received care for medical issues that will require ongoing maintenance care. If you think Jane might be a good fit for your home, we can share a more detailed medical history and potential future care needs.

Complete veterinary records, dating back to the time of rescue, are typically sent via email after an adoption. They can also be sent in advance of an adoption, upon request.

FOSTER TO ADOPT

Jane Goodall is eligible for our limited foster-to-adopt program, which gives approved adopters the chance to welcome a dog into their home and get to know them before finalizing an adoption. The foster-to-adopt period typically lasts 2–4 weeks and follows our standard foster guidelines. Interested adopters should submit an adoption application noting their interest in the program and the specific dog—they can connect with the adoptions team for more details.
