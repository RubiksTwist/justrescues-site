HISTORY

Dolly PAWton was at a small shelter in Texas, at risk of euthanasia, when our team received an urgent call to help. She traveled to Brooklyn in mid-September 2026.

DETAILED PROFILE

Dolly PAWton has a signature walk: a tidy little prance, perfected to a science, with a hop over every sidewalk ledge for good measure. She’s a small, gentle senior with low energy and a curious streak. Put her somewhere new and she’ll investigate every corner rather than hide under a blanket.

She’s calm, even a little aloof, with new people at first, possibly because she doesn’t hear well. Spend a couple of minutes beside her and she warms right up. With her own people, she’s turning into a full-on snuggler: start petting her and she leans in hard for scratches, then flops over for more. She doesn’t always respond to her name, but a hand signal brings her strutting over. She’s been around kids and done fine, though gentle ones are a must for a dog her size.

Her social life is a highlight. She and her foster’s young puppy love racing to the door and chasing each other around, and she stays calm even when he gets rowdy. She lives with cats and is largely unbothered by them, and she took busy adoption events full of people and dogs in stride.

She’s a dream on leash and happiest on easy-paced trots. She sleeps peacefully in her crate at night; during the day she can get a little fussy, but blankets and a treat settle her. House training is still a work in progress, with accidents most likely overnight, and she does best with trips outside every couple of hours.

Off duty, she rolls around on pillows and blankets, sometimes right off them, then simply stays put. And snacks? She found the puppy’s peanut butter jar within minutes of walking in the door. Priorities: firmly in order.
