HISTORY

Originally from the USVI, Tucks was attacked by a group of dogs and abandoned by his owner, arriving at the shelter in critical condition and nearly septic. His injuries required four days of emergency veterinary care, and he has since healed well. He traveled to Brooklyn in late March 2026.

DETAILED PROFILE

Tucks is a big, handsome young dog who is completely convinced he’s still a puppy, and he’s not entirely wrong. He’s warm, goofy, and affectionate, the kind of dog who parks himself beside you while you work and rests his head in your lap like it’s his job. Take a shower and he’ll be waiting on the bath mat when you’re done.

He takes squeaky toys very seriously (they rarely survive), and he once carried a teddy bear he found in the grass all the way home. He’ll also scoop up a stray tennis ball and carry it proudly for the rest of the walk. He loves a good window, sometimes pushing his head through the curtain to supervise the street. Squirrels and rats get his full attention; one evening he dove headfirst into a pepper planter in pursuit.

He’s a moderate-energy dog who is happiest, and calmest, after a real walk. He’s smart, knows sit, down, stay, and come, and sits politely to wait for dinner. He’s house trained, settles in his crate (a treat helps), and is pretty good on leash. He’s nearly given up jumping on people and checks in when he hears his name.

Tucks is gentle and sweet with people and has been good with kids he meets out and about. He’s selective with dogs: smaller dogs are usually no big deal, but larger dogs can get him worked up. He’d do best as the only dog, and any dog sibling would need a careful match. His experience with cats isn’t known yet.

He’ll shine with an adopter who has experience or is eager to learn, and who’ll keep up the structure that has helped him grow. Bonus points if you don’t mind company outside the shower.

MEDICAL HISTORY

The dogs in our care are up-to-date on vaccinations, spayed or neutered (when old enough), microchipped, and given a general health check. Immediate medical needs and medical history will be addressed with the adopter prior to adoption. Complete vet records will be sent shortly after adoption.
