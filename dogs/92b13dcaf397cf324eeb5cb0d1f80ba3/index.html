Bearstein came to ACC as a stray. Because of this, we do not know how he is in a home environment. During his time in the care center, Bearstein has shown high levels of fear, resulting in him growling and snapping. When on a straycation, he showed significant separation anxiety, resulting in him scratching and biting doors that separated him from people along with constant barking and crying in the crate. He is noted to charge and bark at people approaching him when he is with his person. He is noted to be high energy and have a high prey drive towards small animals, and with his strength he is hard to redirect. Bearstein is described as an incredibly sweet dog who loves to give kisses and cuddle. We recommend placement with a New Hope partner who can provide any necessary behavior modification (force-free, positive reinforcement-based) and re-evaluate behavior in a stable home environment before placement into a permanent home.
