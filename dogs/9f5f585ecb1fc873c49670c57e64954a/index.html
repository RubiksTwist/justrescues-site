I'll play with a dog friend, then happily chill beside them. Toss something for me to chase, or hand over a candy cane squeaky chew, and I'm set. The leash and I are still negotiating. Pets, though? MY FOSTER SAYS: "She was so scared to go down stairs and with some coaxing she's now a stair master!"

HISTORY

Honey Badger is from Georgia, where she was part of a litter of puppies headed to rescue until a local woman adopted her as a pet for her teenage grandson. About six months later, the family no longer wanted to care for a young dog, and she was taken back into rescue care. She traveled to Brooklyn in mid-October 2026.

DETAILED PROFILE

Honey Badger's foster sums her up in two words: polite and gentle. She's a young, medium-to-large brindle girl with a goofy, slightly clumsy streak and a real desire to please the people she's with. Stairs scared her at first. With a little coaxing, she now takes them like a pro, which says a lot about how willing she is to try new things.

She has moderate to active energy and loves a game, especially anything she gets to chase. A candy cane squeaky chew is her current favorite. She plays nicely with her foster's dog, breaks off when asked, and is just as happy to relax nearby without playing. With new people she's friendly and curious, more interested in a polite sniff than a fuss. Her experience with kids is limited, but she's been fine around school-age kids. Cats are still unknown.

She's new to her foster home and still getting used to city life, so early signs are encouraging but still coming into focus. She took to her crate surprisingly easily and stays relaxed there even with distractions outside. House training is close, with an occasional accident while everyone learns the routine. Leash walking is the main homework: sidewalks are new to her and she pulls, but she's started checking in with her handler and responds well to her name. She knows sit and down.

Stairs: conquered. Next opponent: the sidewalk. Smart money's on Honey Badger.

MEDICAL HISTORY

The dogs in our care are up-to-date on vaccinations, spayed or neutered (when old enough), microchipped, and given a general health check. Immediate medical needs and medical history will be addressed with the adopter prior to adoption. Complete vet records will be sent shortly after adoption.
