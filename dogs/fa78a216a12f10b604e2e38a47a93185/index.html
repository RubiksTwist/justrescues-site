Pre-Screener Form

Quinn is at risk due to behavioral concerns. Quinn has been highly social with handlers, enjoys playing with toys, and readily engages in pattern games and training sessions. He has displayed a low threshold for arousal that has manifested as intense leash biting and persistent jumping on handlers/staff, which has resulted in bite incidents that have broken the skin. The first incident occurred during an interaction, and the second was during a walk. Quinn intially could be redirected with treats and toys, but his arousal continued to escalate, which has made leashing him from the kennel and out on walks difficult. Medically, Quinn is apparently healthy.

Quinn was surrendered to the care centers as a stray, with no known history. During his time at the care center, he has shown social behavior with staff and has been an active and eager participant in training sessions, particularly enjoying running on the slat mill. Quinn has shown high arousal behavior in the care center, jumping up and leash biting when being walked. In one instance, this escalated to redirection onto the handler, resulting in multiple bite-and-releases and a long scratch to the handler's arm, although it is not clear whether this was caused by a bite or by Quinn's nails. In an interaction with a potential adopter, a treat was held in a closed fist above Quinn's head, and Quinn grazed the person's thumb with his tooth as he tried to take it, breaking skin. Quinn has also shown defensive behavior in high stress situations; while running on the slat mill, he caught a nail and began to thrash. As the handler attempted to steady him by grabbing his collar, he turned his head and bit their hand, a bite and release which did not break skin. Due to these concerns, we recommend that Quinn be placed in an adult-only home through a New Hope Rescue partner, that can allow him the time and space to decompress and acclimate, and provide any necessary (force-free, positive-reinforcement based) behavior modification.
