Submit an adoption application a t socialteesnyc.org Our Team Says: "This pup was rescued from a hoarding situation in PR - involving about 40 dogs. Due to this these pups are on the shyer side and need patient people who will give them time to get used to normal life. They are very sweet dogs: just need more time and patience. We love these dogs - we've rescued about 15 of them and they have all been a joy." Their Foster Parent Says: " Chester has been great. He is slowly progressing. Very slowly, but I notice him come out of his shell tiny bits at a time more everyday. He is the easiest dog. He is fully housebroken, never barks. My parent's fostered and ended up adopting a dog a couple of years ago that is a lot like him. She would barely look you in the eyes, didn't want to be pet, would stay in her crate or bed pretty much all the time, but I watched her come out of her shell the same way Chester is. I think he has a long road ahead before he seems more comfortable with people, but I can already tell that in the few weeks I have had him that he is learning that I am part of his pack and trusts me. Just this morning he got out of his bed on his own and came to find me putting on my shoes because I was about to take him outside. I clipped on his leash and only slightly had to coax him to go out. Normally I have to go to his bed and pick him up or he won't go. He still is spending 100% of his time in his little bed, aside from when I walk him, but I believe in time he will join me in the other room here and there. I see the curiosity and bravery trying to push through. When we are on walks he is curious about other dogs and wants to socialize with them. He will also stop and look up at people as they walk by like he is curious about them. My husband is traveling right now so I think it is possible that he is also checking to see if any of those people are him. He will definitely need a home and parent that has a lot of patience with him and knows that it will take him a long time to ever want to cuddle (if ever). Like I was mentioning with my parent's dog, only now a few years later will she choose to jump on the couch and sit with me and my husband and let us pet her, and when we go home to my parent's she comes to greet us now, but that took a while. He seems to have a very similar demeanor. Extremely sweet, but very cautious. I also think that a home with another dog that is trained and sweet would be really good for him. I think it would help show him that it is okay and safe." Level of dog ownership experience required: BEGINNER – “Beginner” doesn’t necessarily mean easy as all dogs will require time, work, and patience, but we predict this dog will be low on the challenge scale because of their breed/age combo. Potentially a good fit for someone who has relevant dog care experience (fostering, dog sitting) and is looking to adopt their first pup. Tolerance to City He is slowly but surely becoming acclimated to the city, however he is easily scared by noises around him. This has gotten better in the time we have had him, but I think it will be a long road for him. Potty Training Yes. The only accident was the very first day we had him (to be expected) none since then. Comfort level with strangers Generally speaking he is a wallflower. We have noticed that with females he seems to be more inclined to say hello if they want to engage with him. Fortunately even though myself and my husband are cis men, he has taken to us and seems to seek us for comfort in various situations. Comfort level with kids It is hard to say how he will be with kids as not only do we not have any, but he hasn’t encountered many on walks or in our building that he’s really gotten close to. He is SO gentle that I am inclined to say he would be good and very tolerant with kids, however it is important to know that I don’t think he would enjoy playing with kids in any way. Social behavior with other dogs Our building is very dog friendly so there are lots of dogs in the building and we encounter many on our walks. For the most part he ignores them or isn’t too interested in them, but every once in a while he is curious. Generally speaking, when he is around other dogs he’s not at all aggressive. Similarly to kids, I am inclined to say that he would be good in a home with other dogs and it might help him come out of his shell a bit more. With other animals Would be fine in a home with a cat Mouthy Not at all. He doesn’t really play and has never shown any sign of aggression or a snarl or anything like that. Alone time / Separation He is totally happy being left alone. Sadly I almost think sometimes he would prefer it, which is hard for us because our frenchie that we had for15 years was like velcro and always wanted to be around us and sitting with us, cuddling and getting pets. That said, I am of course happy he doesn’t seem at all anxious when we leave him alone. Leash Overall he is good on the leash. Sometimes he does pull and zig-zags around. This mainly seems to happen when he has been spooked. Otherwise he is pretty good on the leash and understands what’s happening. Cuddles As I previously mentioned, he is mostly disinterested with cuddling. A few times I Brough him into my lap and pet him and scratched him and I think he did enjoy it and made sure not to restrain or hold him there at all so he felt trapped, but if I stopped petting him pretty soon after he would leave to go back to his bed. He still hasn’t opted for sitting with us or staying after the pets stop. Maybe in time this will change. My parents had a foster that they adopted that shows a lot of the same behaviors and she now comes up to us when we come home and wants our pets and she will sometimes even jump onto the couch just to sit with us, which she doesn’t seem to do with anyone else. I am hoping he will get to this stage too, but I think it will take him a long time to get that comfortable with someone. Jumping to greet Definitely not. He isn’t looking to greet anyone. He is a shy shy shy guy Crate training He is totally crate trained and as previously mentioned he much prefers the crate. In fact we had to move it once while he was out on a walk and when he came back he went straight over to the corner where the crate was and waited for us to put it back in its correct spot. Once the crate was back in the normal spot he went right back in on his own and curled up in his blankets. Medical status: Healthy as far as we know. Up to date on their DHPP, Bordetella, and Rabies vaccines; heartworm tested if coming from Tennessee or Puerto Rico; spayed/neutered; microchipped; treated with dewormer upon intake How did this dog end up with Social Tees? Most of our dogs come via partner groups who help us save them from shelters where they are at risk of euthanasia in Tennessee, Los Angeles, and Puerto Rico. There are very few spay/neuter resources there and many more animals in those areas than there are people to adopt them. Rescue groups like ours bring them to the northeast where the desire for pets like these allows us to save their lives. PLEASE NOTE: We cannot guarantee any breed mix, exact age, or full grown size (if the dog is under a year old). We do our best with very educated guesses.
