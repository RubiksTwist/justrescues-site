Submit an adoption application a t socialteesnyc.org Thier Foster Says: Sutton is the gentlest, sweetest horse boy you've ever seen. He's a big guy at 70 pounds, but he folds himself into the cutest little ball and wants nothing more than your love. When he's ready for pets, he nuzzles right into your lap, and he melts when you talk to him, touch him, and tell him he's a good boy (which he is, constantly). He's shy for the first couple of minutes with new people, gets his sniffs in, and then turns into a total cuddle bug with everyone. After just one week with me, Sutton has his routine down. He's up around 7 for a long morning walk, has a shorter stroll at lunch, another long walk in the evening, and a quick lap around the block before bed. By day three he was letting me know when he needed an extra potty trip. On walks he's a dream: he barely pulls, and he keeps glancing back at me to make sure he's going the right way. The sniffs and the new friends along the route are his favorite part. He's polite with other dogs, choosy about who he plays with, and smart enough to steer clear of any dog that seems unfriendly. City noise is still new to him (loud trucks can startle him), but he's settling in a little more every day. At home, Sutton is the ultimate work-from-home buddy. He plops down on his bed next to me and naps until it's time to go out, and some days he's so cozy I have to coax him up for his walk. He follows me from room to room because he just wants to be near his person. He ignores the couch but loves the foot of the bed at night, where he's a very considerate co-sleeper with no sprawling. He doesn't chew anything, doesn't guard his food or toys, and stays quiet when the doorbell rings. He isn't very food-motivated (peanut butter is a hard pass so far) and tends to save his meals for dinner, but he responds beautifully to his name, verbal cues, and praise. He's sweet, sensitive, and loyal, and he'll make someone a wonderful companion/" Level of dog ownership experience required: INTERMEDIATE – This dog will need significant structure and plenty of daily exercise, training, and mental stimulation – 90 consecutive minutes of movement in the morning plus potty break walks and brain games throughout the day. Most of our dogs come via partner groups who help us save them from shelters where they are at risk of euthanasia in Tennessee, Los Angeles, and Puerto Rico. There are very few spay/neuter resources there and many more animals in those areas than there are people to adopt them. Rescue groups like ours bring them to the northeast where the desire for pets like these allows us to save their lives.
