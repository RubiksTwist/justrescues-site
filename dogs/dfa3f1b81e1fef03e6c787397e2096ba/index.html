ABOUT ME: After every bathroom break, I kick up dirt and grumble like a grizzly, because a job well done deserves a show. Indoors I’m a cuddle bug with a cushy crate mat, a pile of stuffies, and a window to watch. I just got here, so new faces get a slow once-over, and sidewalk dogs get a piece of my mind. Once I’ve picked my person, though, I’m a lapdog through and through.

MY FOSTER SAYS: “Backpack is a wonderful combination of sugar, some spice, and everything nice. I’d die for him.”

HISTORY

Backpack was found tied up outside our rescue partner’s offices in Dallas, Texas, freshly bathed and left with toys and treats. He was taken to a local shelter for medical care and traveled to Brooklyn in late October 2026.

DETAILED PROFILE

Backpack has a signature move. Every time he finishes his business outside, he kicks up dirt (or just air) while grumbling and growling like a grizzly defending his title, and he keeps it up for an impressively long time. His foster finds it hilarious. Indoors, the drama melts away: he’s a snuggly little guy who curls up with his people, claims the window seat, and treats a cushy crate mat as his personal den, sometimes putting himself to bed.

He’s affectionate and attached once he knows you, but he’s still very new to Brooklyn and cautious with strangers, so he does best when new people let him make the first move, though he generally warms up quickly. He’s also particular about handling, and he may put his mouth on a hand, without biting, if a bath or grooming goes on longer than he’d like. Because of that mouthiness, we’re recommending a home without young children for now, until we know more about how he does with kids.

He lives with three other dogs and ranges from unbothered to genuinely friendly with them. When play gets too rowdy, he tells them to back off or takes a break. He passed a cat test and can live with a cat. On leash it’s a different story: he can be loudly reactive to other dogs and some people, though he does better walking alongside a dog buddy and when he keeps moving.

He knows sit, place, stay, off, and come, and he’s eager to please. House training is close; the bigger project is his enthusiasm for leg-lifting on things like lobby furniture. He’s comfortable in his crate most of the time but can get vocal when he’s ready to come out.

Fair warning: the post-potty victory dance comes standard.

MEDICAL HISTORY

The dogs in our care are up-to-date on vaccinations, spayed or neutered (when old enough), microchipped, and given a general health check. Immediate medical needs and medical history will be addressed with the adopter prior to adoption. Complete vet records will be sent shortly after adoption.
