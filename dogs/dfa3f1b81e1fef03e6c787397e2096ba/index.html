ABOUT ME: After every bathroom break, I kick up dirt and grumble like a grizzly, because a job well done deserves a show. Indoors I’m a cuddle bug with a cushy crate mat, a pile of stuffies, and a window to watch. I just got here, so new faces get a slow once-over, and sidewalk dogs get a piece of my mind. Once I’ve picked my person, though, I’m a lapdog through and through.

MY FOSTER SAYS: "I have heard his muffled barks while he's sleeping - wondering if he's dreaming about meeting other dogs on a walk :). He is really a total lovebug and the easiest boy in the home."

HISTORY

Backpack was found tied up outside our rescue partner’s offices in Dallas, Texas, freshly bathed and left with toys and treats. He was taken to a local shelter for medical care and traveled to Brooklyn in late October 2026.

DETAILED PROFILE

Backpack has a signature move. Every time he finishes his business outside, he kicks up dirt while grumbling like a grizzly defending his title. Indoors, the drama melts away. He's a snuggly lovebug who chases a squeaky toy, plays a mean game of tug, and sleeps best wrapped in a blanket. Some nights he lets out muffled barks in his sleep, presumably telling off a dream dog.

He's affectionate and attached once he knows you, but new people are a bigger deal. Right now he barks and lunges at visitors and at some strangers on the street, so he does best with people who give him space and let him decide when to say hello. Baths, grooming, and putting on his harness or leash can make him growly, wiggly, and mouthy (no biting). For now, we're recommending a home without young children. Small-dog people will read him easily.

At home he's easygoing with other animals. He has lived with three dogs, from unbothered to genuine friends, and tells a rowdy playmate to back off when needed. He lives with a cat now and has started giving it more room. On leash it's a different story: he's strongly reactive to other dogs, barking and lunging even at a distance, and can be hard to calm once he's worked up. He does much better walking with a small group of dogs, and a slow, patient introduction to a neighbor's dog ended in a calm hello. His adopter should be ready to keep working on this.

He's settled into a comfortable routine, rarely has accidents (though he may test the furniture with a leg lift), stays calm when gated in at home, and knows a few cues with more in the works.

Fair warning: the post-potty victory dance comes standard.

MEDICAL HISTORY

The dogs in our care are up-to-date on vaccinations, spayed or neutered (when old enough), microchipped, and given a general health check. Immediate medical needs and medical history will be addressed with the adopter prior to adoption. Complete vet records will be sent shortly after adoption.
