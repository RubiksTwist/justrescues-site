Submit an adoption application a t socialteesnyc.org Our Team Says: " We get to stroll around the neighborhood with this snuggle buddy most mornings — his foster mom, who runs @elsewhereespresso , lives near our office and does the same daily community garden circuit we’re on. What a fun start to each day. As soon as Goose sees someone he recognizes, the gentle wiggles start ramping up. Even if he doesn’t know you, he’ll get excited at the chance to meet a new friend. This slight 1-year-old terrier mix (only 30 lbs) is an easy going, joyful boy. After an initial adjustment period from Tennessee pup to NYC boy, he’s proven himself potty trained, fine home alone, confident, affectionate, awesome with other dogs, a gentle treat taker, quiet, and great on leash. His only flaw, if you can call it that, is he’ll try to drag you into an alternate universe if he sees a squirrel. May not be a great match with cats, and working up to off leash activity will need to be a careful journey. Goose needs a solid 90 minute walk each morning and is mister mellow once he’s had that exercise." His Foster Family Says: “Goose is a sweet little cuddle bug that does well when he gets attention and a long walk in the morning. He loves to say hi to everyone and is polite with other dogs too. He hasn’t had any accidents inside the house since his second day here and now seems confident peeing and pooping outside. He is definitely interested in squirrels and cats and the occasional bird or rat, so that is something to be mindful of. Leaving him alone has so far not been a problem. Otherwise he spends a lot of time snoozing on the couch or in bed. He’s a wonderful, special little creature.” TOLERANCE TO CITY City boy – Doing so well here! Up to date on their DHPP, Bordetella, and Rabies vaccines; heartworm tested if coming from Tennessee or Puerto Rico; spayed/neutered; microchipped; treated with dewormer upon intake How did this dog end up with Social Tees? Most of our dogs come via partner groups who help us save them from shelters where they are at risk of euthanasia in Tennessee, Los Angeles, and Puerto Rico. There are very few spay/neuter resources there and many more animals in those areas than there are people to adopt them. Rescue groups like ours bring them to the northeast where the desire for pets like these allows us to save their lives.
